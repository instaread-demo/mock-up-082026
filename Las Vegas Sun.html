NV Energy asks regulators to lower Southern Nevada electric bills starting in October

Utility says residential customers would see average savings of about $3 to $5 a month

Exterior view of the NV Energy building Monday, Oct. 20, 2014, in Las Vegas. Photo by: Steve Marcus

By Las Vegas Sun Staff (contact)

Wednesday, Aug. 19, 2026 | 10:45 a.m.

NV Energy has filed a request with state regulators to lower electric rates for Southern Nevada customers, a move the company says reflects declining fuel and energy market costs.

The utility filed the application today with the Public Utilities Commission of Nevada. If approved, the filing would reduce NV Energy’s annual revenues by approximately $100.2 million and lower bills for customers across all major rate classes, effective Oct. 1.

For a typical residential customer, the change would translate to a monthly bill decrease of about $5.01, or 3.63%.

“Keeping energy affordable while maintaining a safe and reliable electric system is one of our highest priorities,” said Brandon Barkhuff, president and CEO of NV Energy. “As fuel and energy market costs change, these periodic adjustments help make sure customers benefit when those costs decline.”

Proposed decreases by rate class include:

• Residential service: 3.63% (about $5.01 per month)

• Residential multifamily: 4.04% (about $3.14 per month)

• General service: 3.95% (about $3 per month)

• All customer classes combined: 4.10%

NV Energy generates much of the electricity it delivers to customers through company-owned power plants, supplemented by purchases from the regional energy market when demand requires it. The company says it passes fuel and purchased-power costs directly to customers with no markup and reviews those costs periodically against the rates it already collects.

The PUCN is scheduled to hold an evidentiary hearing Monday in Las Vegas on a separate NV Energy rate proceeding — the company’s annual deferred energy accounting adjustment for 2025 fuel and purchased-power costs — which, if approved as filed, would raise Southern Nevada residential rates by about $0.73 per month.

The PUCN may set rates higher or lower than those requested by the company. The commission’s regulatory staff, the Bureau of Consumer Protection, and other parties will review the filing before any decision is reached.
